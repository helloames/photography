Second Week at BlackRock

June 21, 2026

I finished my second week of the BlackRock internship! Overall, things are slowly progressing: since the firm is so expansive, it has been taking a long time to get permissions and access to codebases for my work. Work has been great, though—I really like my manager, and everyone on the team has been incredibly welcoming. The culture is excellent, and it has been exciting to continue meeting people across the firm.

In numbers, this week, I ran 16.15 miles, which is comparable to last week. Between work and spending time with friends outside of work, it was difficult to balance fitness with increasing my mileage, which was a goal that I mentioned last week. Outside of exercise, however, I have been eating very well. My notable restaurants this week were Nubiani and Sendo in Midtown. The former is a popular Korean barbecue spot, and their banchan dishes were some of the best I have ever had. The restaurant also had a lovely selection of meat cuts, and the chefs did a fantastic job grilling everything tableside. Sendo was my first experience trying omakase, and it was quite the treat. I went with a few other BlackRock interns, and their Hokkaido omakase set included ten pieces of nigiri, two hand rolls, and a kaisendon—a rice bowl topped with assorted seafood.

This week, I also visited Flushing, Queens, for the first time. A few friends and I decided to do a food crawl, and we spent an afternoon eating our way through the neighborhood, stopping at various drink shops, bakeries, and dessert spots. Everything was excellent. We quickly realized that Flushing offers food quality comparable to Manhattan, but without Manhattan-level prices. I can definitely see myself making the trip out there again this summer.

Outside of work, I have been trying to make an effort to see my friends. One of my best friends from Duke visited me this past weekend, and we spent a lot of time walking around Central Park, the West Side Highway, and SoHo. The weather was amazing—I think New York City is best experienced when it is around seventy degrees and sunny. We also visited the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum. The museum itself is an architectural marvel designed by Frank Lloyd Wright. The building consists of a continuous spiral ramp surrounding an open-air atrium, creating a museum experience that feels very different from most traditional galleries. The artwork lines the walls along the spiral, and it is about a quarter-mile walk from the ground floor to the top on the seventh floor.

Personally, I think the art at the Guggenheim was a little too modern for my taste, but I loved how interactive and immersive the museum felt. There also was not a huge amount of artwork compared to places like the Met or MoMA, which are impressive because of the sheer scale of their collections. I think that is largely a result of the building's unique architecture—the open-air design means there is less wall space available for exhibits. Even so, I would still highly recommend visiting. Having been to the Met and MoMA before, I think the Guggenheim is surprisingly underrated and well worth experiencing, even if only for the building itself.

Regarding the Knicks' NBA Finals victory that I wrote about last week, Manhattan has finally started to calm down a bit, even with the FIFA World Cup taking place this summer. The Knicks victory parade looked amazing, but I do not know anyone who actually attended. I suspect that having a corporate internship interferes with that sort of weekday celebration. It probably also helps that many of the World Cup matches are being played across the river in New Jersey rather than directly in New York City. The city still feels lively, but it is noticeably less chaotic than it was during the height of Knicks fever.
