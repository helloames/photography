First Week at BlackRock

June 14, 2026

My internship at BlackRock started this week! Monday and Tuesday were fully dedicated to orientation, but today (Wednesday), I finally got my work laptop set up and met my manager! I'm really excited about the work, if only because this is my first internship where the engineers are assigned MacBooks as their work laptops. I also had a few work-related social events during my weekday evenings this week: beyond the useful networking, I've also learned that I am deeply introverted. I always need a few hours of silence every evening after work to recover my social battery.

In numbers, this week, I ran 16.11 (mostly West Side Highway and Battery Park). This was slightly more than last week, and I'm excited to increase my mileage while living in the city. Also, I've been eating out a lot less this week simply because I've been so busy. However, the notable places I've been to are 1) YONO Hudson Yards and 2) Van Leeuwen's in Hudson Yards. YONO was lovely – an intern friend and I both ordered coconut matcha waters, and it was a nice boost of caffeine after a very hectic day at internship orientation and felt lighter and more refreshing than a latte. Regarding Van Leeuwen's ice cream, I've tried several flavors already: honeycomb, dark chocolate cherry, and mango sticky rice. I think mango sticky rice is my favorite – mostly because it's a lovely dessert and a unique ice cream flavor that I can't find elsewhere, and it reminds me of home!

I also realized this week that some NYC restaurants add a service fee for eating outside. One of my Duke friends (who is also working in the city!) and I recently had dinner at Jupiter, an Italian restaurant and bar in the Rockefeller Center. We were walk-ins and asked to be seated at the bar, and when we ordered from the food (non-alcohol) menu at the bar, the menu said that there would have been a 20% service fee for eating at the outdoor seating! I was pretty surprised – it was akin to culture shock. In a similar vein, my roommate and I also realized a few days ago that most grocery stores charge anywhere from a few cents to almost a dollar if you do not bring your own reusable grocery bags! Since then, we've adapted the practice of carrying reusable tote bags everywhere, but especially when we plan on grocery shopping.

It's also been a crazy few weeks in NYC because of the Knicks vs. Spurs in the NBA Finals. To some extent, I had really hoped the Knicks wouldn't win while at home (the area around Madison Square Garden, in Midtown of NYC, is actually so crazy before and after a Knicks game and taking them becomes more intimidating). I'm very lucky that this wish was actually granted – the Knicks ended up winning in 5 games, and while in San Antonio. NYC was very hyped up for the win; there was screaming and cheering on the street I live on, 3rd Ave, until 3:30 AM-ish after the game. I think it's because I live in the East Village, which is where a lot of young, 20-somethings live, and there are many sports bars in the neighborhood. To some extent, I also think I didn't buy into the Knicks hype because I was a little jealous of the whole framework of the NBA Finals – the Knicks have 7 games/chances to get 4 wins to win the whole championship, but when Duke plays in March Madness, it's only single-elimination? How unfortunate! I also think that most of NYC is band-wagoning with the Knicks, since the prices of fan merchandise have skyrocketed recently, and the street vendors in NYC are always selling Knicks hats and t-shirts.
