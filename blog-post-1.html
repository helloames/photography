Last Week Before Work

June 14, 2026

My internship at BlackRock does not start until June 8th, so here is a blog of my last week before work! I tried to enjoy the city as much as I could.

In numbers, I ran 13.02 miles around NYC this week – mostly around Central Park, Brooklyn Bridge, and West Side High Way. I also did two workouts at Barry's NoHo! Barry's is a HIIT workout with running and circuit work.

Outside of fitness, I met up with plenty of friends. Since this is the summer of frozen yogurt, we tried three different frozen yogurt places: Myka, Mimi's, and Culture! I think Mimi's was my favorite: of the three, Mimi's (in Nolita) has indoor seating and the best selection and value of toppings. Myka (in West Village) has no indoor seating, and Culture (in Greenwich Village) has the most expensive toppings – they charge $1.50 per topping– and crowded indoor seating!

Apart from frozen yogurt, I tried a few other restaurants. Smør in the East Village was my favorite restaurant from this week: it's a Scandinavian brunch spot that I tried with one of my Duke friends who graduated last year. Their pancakes were divine: very light, fluffy, and not too sweet because of the creamy blueberry topping. I also loved Smør's take on smørrebrød – it's an open-sandwhich concept with protein toppings like salmon and pickled herring with a dense, chewy bread as the base.

In terms of actual work, I am still wrapping up a few miscellaneous research projects for Argus Lab, a Duke ECE research group I'm part of. We had two papers we started last semester, and with upcoming IEEE deadlines, we're working hard to finish them over the summer! I tried to frontload all of the project work I had this week since my 9-5 internship starts on June 8th. I've also been practicing my coding skills with leetcode, especially if I decide to re-recruit for a new grad job this fall. The leetcode problems have been quite tricky lately!

Overall, it was a very fulfilling week! I enjoyed the NYC weather, food, and the new running routes. It's been lovely to meet up with friends as they slowly trickle into the city for their new grad jobs and internships, and I have enjoyed catching up with my alumni friends who have been living in the city. Funnily enough, I don't think Manhattan is actually that big – it feels like an extension of the Duke bubble, mostly because I keep running into acquaintances (especially from my freshman year) at the most random places: at the gym, brunch, workout classes, etc. I'm very excited for my 9-5 to start; I think it will add more structure to my life! I also think I'm a little nervous; I've never worked at an asset manager before, much less the world's biggest asset manager.
